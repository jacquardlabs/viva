# Pre-mortem — Preferences inspector: view and manage learned preferences from the browser UI

- Branch: epic/reviewer-experience--prefs-inspector
- SHA: 551139a
- Date: 2026-08-03

| # | Lane | Failure mode | Detection hint |
|---|------|--------------|----------------|
| 1 | technical | The design commits badges to render linked "from first paint" by awaiting the preferences fetch alongside round data, but says nothing about round 2+. Cards rebuild from the SSE `round` handler (`server.py:3336-3345`), which replaces `REVIEW_DATA` and re-renders without any fetch — if the preferences list isn't cached client-side and reused there, every round-2+ `kind:"preference"` badge silently degrades to plain text, breaking acceptance criterion 4 on every round after the first with no error. | Drive a round-2 rewrite where a section still carries a `kind:"preference"` annotation (SKILL.md:301, opt-in on later rounds); confirm the jump control still renders on the rebuilt card, not just on the round-1 render. |
| 2 | technical | `POST /preferences/mute` writes the store through `preferences.py`'s `_write` (`scripts/preferences.py:179-183`), a plain `path.write_text` with no temp-and-rename, while `server.py` already owns `_atomic_write` (`server.py:3740`). A crash or full disk mid-write truncates `.viva/preferences.json` — producing exactly the corruption the design's own journey step 8 names as a hazard to degrade from. | Grep the mute handler for its write call: reaching `preferences._write` or a bare `write_text` on `.viva/preferences.json` is the defect; `_atomic_write` is the pass. |
| 3 | technical | The panel's whole list is the `aria-live` region (journey step 4). If rows are rendered into an already-live container at open time, a screen reader announces every preference on open, not just the one status change after a mute — the announcement surface the design deliberately avoided in `#stats-area` reappears inside the panel. | Open the panel with 5+ preferences in the store under VoiceOver/NVDA: a full-list read-out on open, rather than silence until the first mute, is the failure. Scoping `aria-live` to a one-line status element instead of the `<ul>` is the fix shape. |
| 4 | technical | The new bottom-bar control lands inside `#stats-area`, which already carries `aria-live="polite"` (`server.py:1703`). Placement is settled (decision prefs-inspector-2) and the static-label choice is the mitigation — but any later change that bakes a count or state into that button's own text double-announces on every counter update. | Read the button's emitted text content: any interpolated value (a preference count, "3 muted", an open/closed word) inside `#stats-area` is the regression. |
| 5 | product | Mute is one-way from the UI (decision prefs-inspector-1) and the panel shows muted rows read-only with no path forward. A reviewer who mis-clicks Mute sees the row flip and the button vanish, with nothing on screen naming the `preferences.py set --status standing` recovery — a dead end inside the surface whose whole premise is "without leaving the tab". | Mute a preference in the panel and read the muted row: no static text naming the un-mute command anywhere in the panel is the failure. Static copy, not a control — it adds no route and no state. |
| 6 | product | Muting does not un-flag the section already on screen (journey step 7, correct by design — pre-flight is next-session). The reviewer mutes, closes the panel, sees the badge unchanged, and concludes the mute failed — then re-mutes or drops to the terminal to check, which is the context switch this story exists to remove. | Dogfood: mute a preference whose badge is visible on an active card, then watch whether the reviewer re-opens the panel or the terminal to confirm. Absence of any "takes effect next session" copy on the muted row is the design-side tell. |
